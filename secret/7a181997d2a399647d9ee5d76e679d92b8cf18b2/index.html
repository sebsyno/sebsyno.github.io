Executive summary — five findings (updated after the 240-cell product audit)
1The incumbent tier has converged on one interchangeable playbook — one platform, lifecycle trust, PE-driven M&A, parallel geographic expansion — and the cell-by-cell audit shows the claims often outrun the product: transaction monitoring was the most over-claimed capability in the set (five vendors corrected), and only 2 of 16 actually ship the agent tooling the whole field markets.
2Four durable sustainable competitive advantages (moats) are visible in the data: network-scale fraud intelligence (buyable only with volume), national / regulatory relationships, independently certified niches — and, surfaced by the audit itself, native stack ownership: roughly a third of 240 reviewed cells changed tier once partner-delivered and roadmap capability was separated from native product, exposing a supply chain (Sumsub supplies Authologic; Regula licenses to 55% of Gartner MQ vendors; Signicat's EUDI and reuse layers run on partners) that procurement diligence is learning to interrogate.
3IDENTT already holds the hardest moat to replicate — production-grade national eID access in the exact layer the market is racing toward for 2027 — and the audit adds a second verified one: zero partner-delivered cells across its entire reviewed column, a stack-control score of 85 (equal to Sumsub, behind only Shufti Pro), and membership in the set's three-vendor on-prem peer group pending one deployment-statement confirmation.
4IDENTT's measured gaps split cleanly in two: a communicable layer (category claim, One Platform packaging, published proof, developer surface, certification narrative) and a breadth position (38.7 on the audited scale) that is forward-loaded, not structural — shipping its three already-announced capabilities (transaction monitoring, QES, EUDI Wallet) carries it across the midline into the sovereign full-stack quadrant, alongside Sumsub and Shufti Pro.
5The close-the-gap plan is therefore a communication build-out plus delivery of an already-committed roadmap — no new product bets required — which remains the most cost-efficient competitive correction available in this deck, and one the 240-cell audit now underwrites with evidence rather than impression.

Methodology & strategic notes — audit status, Regula, transaction monitoring, Signicat, on-prem peer group, Authologic, row coverage
All 16 companies now reflect a corrected fresh-eyes pass against each public site — every cell in this matrix has been individually reviewed and re-scored on the 5-tier scale, with reasoning attached. No interim-mapped cells remain.

Regula — infrastructure note: Regula is a document-forensics and biometric-technology supplier (SDKs and hardware), not a full-stack compliance platform — several rows above reflect that (no AML, KYB, transaction monitoring or signing products). Strategically, this cuts the other way: Regula states that 55% of Gartner MQ IDV vendors license its document/biometric engine, meaning several vendors in this table plausibly run Regula’s technology under the hood. It is simultaneously a Warsaw-office competitor and an infrastructure layer the field builds on — worth carrying into the board narrative as its own point, not just a row of dashes.

Transaction monitoring — row definition note: this row has proven the least consistently defined capability in the table, alongside video-ident and EUDI. idnow, iDenfy and ComplyCube were each corrected on the same distinction: ongoing AML re-screening (watchlists/sanctions/PEP/adverse-media, refreshed continuously) is not the same capability as payment transaction monitoring (detecting suspicious patterns in financial transaction flows). Most vendors in this set have the former; few have the latter. Locking one definition and re-scoring every column against it is planned as part of the final horizontal consistency pass.

Signicat — the EUDI benchmark and a coherent threat, not scattered checkmarks: Signicat’s EUDI wallet connectivity (delivered via a technology partnership with WSO2) and reusable-identity capability (delivered via a partnership with TrustTech) are both genuine, live, customer-facing products — which is why they still clear the bar for a real capability, distinct from Didit’s and iDenfy’s mere eIDAS-alignment messaging with no wallet product at all. But neither is native, in-house engineering; both are corrected to 3rd party accordingly. This sharpens rather than undermines the strategic point for IDENTT’s DACH/France planning: even the vendor with the broadest European eID/EUDI footprint in this set orchestrates its EUDI wallet and reuse capability through partners rather than building it end-to-end in-house — a genuine point of contrast with IDENTT’s own in-house-build model, worth naming explicitly rather than assuming Signicat’s breadth is all proprietary. Signicat’s remaining native strengths — broadest national eID coverage in the set and a crown-jewel QES/signing stack — combine with its partner-orchestrated EUDI/reuse layer into one coherent strategic position: the cloud orchestration hub that already owns European eID, wallet and signing breadth — precisely the space an “Adaptive Guardian” entry into DACH/France would need to contest. Worth presenting to the board as a single strategic threat, not four separate checkmarks.

On-prem — IDENTT’s real peer group: genuine on-prem/self-hosted deployment is rare in this set. Only Regula and Shufti Pro are marked Full; Shufti in particular is arguably IDENTT’s closest competitive analog on deployment — a 100%-in-house stack with on-prem/private-cloud/SaaS options and no third-party dependencies, alongside strong document forensics and liveness certification (iBeta Level 3, the highest in the set). IDENTT’s own on-prem claim is currently marked Partial pending confirmation of an explicit deployment statement (flagged on IDENTT’s own row above). Resolving that would put IDENTT in a clear three-way on-prem peer group with Regula and Shufti Pro — the deployment benchmark set the board should compare IDENTT against, rather than the SaaS-only majority of the table.

Authologic — orchestration hub, not full-stack builder: the pre-correction column showed Authologic at Full across document, biometric, NFC, age, e-signature, EUDI and eID breadth — reading like a broad in-house builder. It is not. OmniID is an identity orchestration layer over 50+ third-party providers; document and biometric verification are supplied primarily by Sumsub, already in this table as a direct competitor. Once native capability is separated from resold capability, Authologic’s genuine strengths narrow to two category-leading, proprietary rows — EUDI/wallet orchestration and national eID breadth (~24 schemes) — plus a distinctive native age/trust-service layer (Zero-Knowledge Proofs, EAA issuance under eIDAS 2.0, its own PSD2 AISP license), with the rest of the stack now marked 3rd party. This is the clearest structural contrast in the entire dashboard for IDENTT’s own positioning: IDENTT builds its document/biometric/liveness/OCR technology in-house (the Regula/Shufti model); Authologic aggregates others’ technology (the Stripe model) — IDENTT could plausibly be one of Authologic’s 50+ providers. Authologic isn’t competing with IDENTT on verification technology at all; it’s competing on orchestration convenience and eID breadth. IDENTT’s counter-position — native in-house technology, on-prem deployment, defense-grade data control — is precisely what a 50-partner orchestration layer is structurally unable to match for banking, government and military buyers.

Row coverage note: this matrix has no row for verifying AI agents (detecting agent-driven traffic, tokenizing agent identity, tying agent actions to a verified human owner) — a distinct, emerging 2026 capability from the agent-integration row already tracked here (MCP/developer tooling). Incode leads on this specific capability (Agentic Identity / Agentic Trust: agent detection, owner authentication, tokenization, audit trail) and it is not currently visible anywhere in this table. Flagged as a candidate 16th row for a future pass rather than added retroactively without auditing all 16 vendors against it.

Horizontal (capability breadth): each of the 15 capability rows contributes Full = 1.0, Partial = 0.5, 3rd party = 0.35 (real capability, discounted for partner-dependency risk), Announced = 0.1, Not evident = 0; summed and normalized. Vertical (stack control): 70% weight on the share of delivered capability that is engineered in-house (3rd-party cells dilute it), plus 30% weight on deployment control (on-prem Full = 100, Partial = 50, SaaS-only = 0). Reading IDENTT's position: it sits in the sovereign-specialist quadrant — stack control of 85, equal to Sumsub and Onfido/Entrust and behind only Shufti Pro (100) — with narrower breadth (38.7). The breadth gap is partly forward-loaded: three of IDENTT's cells are Announced (transaction monitoring, QES, EUDI Wallet). Shipping those three moves IDENTT's breadth score from 38.7 to ≈57, across the midline into the sovereign full-stack quadrant — the same quadrant as Sumsub and Shufti Pro — without a single additional module. The contrast at the opposite corner is the strategic point: Authologic (30.7 / 20.6) delivers through 50+ partners and owns almost none of its verification stack; Signicat's follow-up audit moved six of its fifteen cells to 3rd party (document, biometric, AML, KYB, EUDI, reuse) — dropping its stack control to 31.8, second-lowest in the set and confirming its position as an orchestration hub whose genuinely native layer is eID breadth, QES/Dokobit, VideoID and NFC. Breadth built on partners and breadth built in-house are not the same asset — this chart is where that difference becomes visible.

What it means for IDENTT

The watch-list by entry market, the build-out agenda, and the assets already in hand.

▼ Build-out agenda

  • Claim a category. "AI-based identity verification" describes the product; the market rewards a named space to own — the substance for a distinctive claim (eID-anchored, regulator-grade, 25-year track record) already exists.
  • Create the One Platform narrative. The category-wide packaging standard is a single platform, not a shelf of separate products (trend 02) — and the audit shows IDENTT is unusually well placed to claim it honestly: Vision, Video, Knowledge, Facelab, OCR and the eID layer all run on one engine, with zero third-party-delivered cells in the reviewed matrix, while several rivals' "one platform" stories conceal partner-supplied layers. This is a repackaging move, not a roadmap item: restructure the site and sales materials around one platform with configurable modules, and fold the three Announced capabilities (transaction monitoring, QES, EUDI Wallet) into the same platform story as its committed expansion — which, per Fig.06d, also carries IDENTT across the breadth midline into the sovereign full-stack quadrant once shipped.
  • Publish proof. No quantified outcome, named client or case study is currently public — the client base to source these from is real; the peer tier shows how quickly published proof compounds.
  • Open the technical front door. A public API reference, sandbox and SDK documentation is now standard from the 42-person challengers to the incumbents; closing it is the fastest credibility gain available.
  • Internationalize the public presence. The site currently reads Poland-first (Polish default, KNF references) while operations span 36 countries — surfacing that footprint is a communication task, not a product one.
  • Resolve the certification narrative. Verify iBeta PAD / NIST FRVT status before external use; either certify or reframe around the verified stack (ISO 27001, QSCert eIDAS, annual pen-tests, ETSI 119 461 as roadmap).

▲ Assets in hand

  • First-mover mObywatel production access via Ministry of Digitalization agreement — the exact national-eID moat Signicat and Authologic build across many markets, held at depth in one. Neither names Poland among certified broker schemes at IDENTT's level of access.
  • German heritage, 25+ years. 1999 Hamburg founding, Swiss expansion, Wrocław R&D — a longevity and DACH-credibility story IDnow (2014) and Authologic (2020) cannot match; currently understated.
  • Government & defence credibility. Defence and Security Equipment Catalogue listing, PWCyber partnership, ZPF membership — a public-sector trust stack only Regula and Incode rival, in different geographies.
  • Deployment flexibility. SaaS or fully on-premises — shared only with Regula and Shufti in this set; a genuine differentiator for bank, government and military buyers.
  • Services depth. Implementation workshops, custom integration, compliance guidance, annual pen-testing — white-glove delivery the API-first challengers structurally can't offer.
  • EUDI & AMLR timing. EUDI Wallet is IDENTT's dominant content theme ahead of the 2027 mandate, and AMLA-readiness work is active — the same regulatory wave Signicat and IDnow are monetizing, with the window still open.
  • Scientific Council. Named biometrics, AI-security and deepfake-detection researchers — an R&D-pedigree signal none of the peer tier can show.

The one-line read. IDENTT competes on substance ahead of its communication — technology, trust credentials and regulatory relationships sit in the top tier of this set, while the public positioning, proof points and go-to-market surface are still being built. That distance is not a weakness in the market; it is the recoverable opportunity this dashboard quantifies.

Where to point first. In Western and Central Europe the benchmark to beat is Signicat — its entire thesis is the cross-border European complexity problem IDENTT is built for, and Poland is absent from its certified broker list. In DACH, IDnow is the entrenched incumbent whose own acquisition-driven integration complexity is IDENTT's cleanest wedge ("single engine, no forced migrations"). Authologic is the closest structural rival — same Polish roots, sharper category story, but no biometric-certification answer and a fraction of IDENTT's delivery history. Regula's Warsaw office makes it the priority watch in home-market deals.

The move. Lead with what no direct rival can replicate — production-grade national eID access, a 25-year German-rooted track record, government-and-defence-grade trust credentials, and EUDI-readiness timed to the AMLR 2027 wave — while closing the three fastest communication gaps: a category claim, published client proof, and an open developer front door. Verify the biometric-certification levels before they headline anything external.
